# Examples

Every figure below comes from the
[tutorial notebook](https://github.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/blob/main/examples/01_tutorial.ipynb),
which runs top to bottom with no data dependency. The notebook is generated by
[`examples/build_notebook.py`](https://github.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/blob/main/examples/build_notebook.py),
so the prose, code and figures stay in one reviewable file. To regenerate:

```bash
python examples/build_notebook.py
```

## The coincidence problem

![Cross-correlogram of heat and cold: the disaggregated construction peaks at exactly 1.000, loadcast at the 0.60 asked for](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/07_coincidence.png)

Published multi-carrier data builds every carrier as a fixed scalar times one
shared profile, $P_m(t) = \kappa_m\,p(t)$. The notebook rebuilds that
construction from scratch and measures its heat–cold correlation, which comes
out at 1.000000. `loadcast` puts the peak where you ask.

![Delivered correlation against the requested knob, with the floor set by the shared calendar](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/07_transfer.png)

The carriers share a calendar, so they stay correlated even when their
residuals are independent. The knob sets the residual coupling, and the closed
form maps it to the correlation you actually observe.

## What it produces

![One week of heat, cold and electricity](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/01_one_week.png)

![The profile decomposed into level, calendar, modulation and residual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/02_decomposition.png)

Each carrier is the product
$P_m(t) = L_m \cdot S(t) \cdot M_m(t-\tau_m) \cdot R_m(t-\tau_m)$, where every
factor except the level has mean 1.

## The two constants in the residual

![AR(1) paths with and without the stationary variance factor, and the residual mean with and without the lognormal correction](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/03_two_constants.png)

Left: without the $\sqrt{1-\phi^2}$ factor, the paths take several hundred
hours to reach their own spread. Right: without the $-\sigma^2/2$ correction,
the residual mean grows with $\sigma$, so widening the noise would quietly add
annual energy.

## Process classes

![A fortnight of each of the four process classes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/06_process_classes.png)

![Load duration curves for the four process classes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/06_duration_curves.png)

The flat top is the capacity cap holding the load factor exactly. The on/off
mask gives campaign and batch their long near-zero tail, which spread alone
cannot produce.

## Lag

![Cross-correlograms as the lag between carriers grows](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/08_lag.png)

At lag zero, a lag and a weak coupling look the same. As $\tau$ grows, the
contemporaneous correlation falls towards the calendar floor, which is also
where a small $\rho$ would put it. To tell them apart, look at where the
correlogram peaks, not only how high.

## Resolution

![Two days at hourly, 30-minute and 15-minute resolution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/10_resolution.png)

Energy, load factor and hourly-equivalent persistence are the same on every
grid. The process classes were calibrated on hourly meters, so sub-hourly
detail is extrapolated.

## Waste heat

![Heat demand and waste heat rejected, with the waste trailing by two hours](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/11_waste.png)

Waste heat is a by-product the site rejects, not energy it buys, so it is set
relative to heat with `waste_to_heat`. It gets its own correlation and lag.

## Why not retrain on your own output

![Persistence ratcheting up and spread decaying over repeated refits, while the load factor stays flat](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Matt-haug/loadcast/main/examples/figures/12_degeneration.png)

When you refit a class on its own synthetic output, round after round,
persistence climbs towards a fixed point and the spread drains away. The load
factor is imposed, so it stays flat and gives no warning.
